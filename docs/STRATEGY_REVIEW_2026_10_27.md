# Maxcalibur strategy review — 27 October 2026

Status: research and recommendation only. No new experiment, training, benchmark, model download, public play, reserved-data use or GitHub publication was launched for this review. Existing frozen contracts and results remain unchanged.

## Recommendation

Change the development strategy. Keep the full Showdown harness and F32 checkpoints, pause the opening-search branch, and move to a reusable policy with structured observations and observable history. Establish a stronger external opponent and test a small pretrained transfer path before deciding how much new model code to build. Warm-start from trustworthy demonstrations where useful, then improve through RL against diverse opponents. Measure complete-battle strength and useful experience per wall hour.

The most defensible next step is a bounded integration and representation milestone, not another expansion of F33's value audit. There is no evidence identifying a uniquely optimal architecture or algorithm. This recommendation ranks expected value and engineering risk from the evidence available today.

## What the path has achieved

The October 10 [original handoff](RESEARCH_HANDOFF.md) set the objective of a strong independent Generation 9 Random Battles agent, with a faithful environment and useful policy before search. It anticipated an 8–16-week research/engineering effort, potentially longer. Seventeen calendar days is therefore not an inherently unreasonable elapsed time. Nevertheless, our iteration process has accumulated avoidable cost.

| Stage | What the evidence supports | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Reference, data, restricted JAX and full Showdown tracks | Pinned mechanics, complete teams, separate player observations, legal/rejected request handling, PPO/GAE, checkpoints and substantial replay evidence | Complete accelerated Gen9 simulation or competitive strength |
| [F4](F4_FULL_LEARNING.md) | All three full-battle learning gates passed; roughly 91–93% random, 46–48% base-power and 5–8% tactical wins at the endpoint | Broad strategic competence |
| [F10](F10_LONG_TRANSFER.md) | Learning and retention of finite supervised diagnostics | Rehearsal superiority in battles: all nine rehearsal-versus-plain contrasts were inconclusive |
| F15–F21 | Useful imitation/representation diagnostics | Better teacher agreement did not establish better tactical battle play in [F21](F21_SHARED_BATTLE.md) |
| [F29](F29_NONLINEAR_POLICY.md) | Nonlinear actor intervention learned in all three seeds with matching initial predictions and selected-row/update budgets | Isolation of nonlinearity from added capacity and shared-trunk feedback |
| [F32](F32_OBSERVED_CONTINUATION.md) | Continued nonlinear policies beat controls in all three seeds; tactical wins 87, 85, 84 per 256 versus 23, 35, 33 | Final advantage includes inherited F29 differences; it is not an isolated continuation effect. Additional-training learning passed in only two seeds; tactical was trained on; no held-out-opponent or public strength claim |
| [F33](F33_OPENING_SEARCH.md) | Opening-only search was implemented and replayed faithfully; no benefit gate passed | A verdict on general search or a diagnosis of critic failure; the four-opening/two-replicate value audit is too small |

F32 is meaningful progress: the learned policy now wins about one-third of games against the local tactical opponent it trained against. Its nonlinear hosts improved from 58→87, 63→85 and 40→84 wins per 256 on the fresh continuation panels. There is no established learning plateau or representation ceiling. Further continuation might help; the proposed representation change is a priority judgment about expected progress, not a demonstrated necessity. None of our heuristic win rates converts into ladder Elo.

The [October 25 objective review](OBJECTIVE_REVIEW_2026_10.md) already identified excessive imitation-focused iteration, missing stronger opposition/history and disproportionate verification. F29/F32 improved the direction, but those broader gaps remain. We should implement the strategic change rather than create another chain of tiny diagnostic milestones.

## Why it has felt slow

**Collection dominates the measured production loop.** F32 took 7,584.90 seconds: 7,191.14 seconds in collection (94.8%) and 131.02 seconds in optimizer calls including compilation (1.73%). Collection includes simulator execution, feature encoding, inference, transport and bookkeeping; this does not identify pure simulator time. Making only those optimizer calls free would reduce runtime by 1.73%, a speedup of about 1.018×. [Measured handoff](F32_OBSERVED_CONTINUATION.md)

**Verification almost repeats the entire computation.** F32's exhaustive replay took another 7,194.39 seconds; F33 took 469.42 seconds for production and 474.73 seconds for replay. These measurements exclude additional qualification, review and sealing overhead. They establish roughly a doubling for these two workflows, not a project-wide accounting of all engineering time. Removing replay entirely would save about 49–50% of production-plus-replay time; sampled replay would save less. This is useful iteration relief, not an order-of-magnitude throughput solution. [F32 replay](../artifacts/reviews/f32-observed-continuation/replay/report.json), [F33 replay](../artifacts/reviews/f33-opening-search/replay/report.json)

**The prototype representation has lasted too long.** The current base encoder combines species, ability, item, moves, types and status in 46 signed hash buckets per Pokémon. It retains some revealed information, but has no event sequence or opponent PP-use counters. The added matchup proxies do not restore temporal information and omit important dynamic effects. The actor is a roughly 64k-parameter feedforward model. These are concrete limitations, not proof of the cause of every loss. Increasing training duration cannot restore information omitted from the actor input. [Base encoder](../python/pokemon_full/features.py), [matchup features](../python/pokemon_terminal_matchup/features.py), [nonlinear actor](../python/pokemon_nonlinear_policy/network.py)

**Some infrastructure couples unrelated concerns.** M7 completed its two-hour qualification window, then failed because a sealed predecessor prose file changed `open` to `o|pen`, while all 431 core source/configuration files matched. That failure must remain recorded, but future runs should not depend on mutable navigation prose. F30's worker timeout was a separate genuine runtime failure; F32 completion does not explain its cause. [M7 disposition](M7_LIVE_TRAINING.md), [F32 scope](F32_OBSERVED_CONTINUATION.md)

The correctness work was valuable: hidden information, request handling and learner consistency are real failure modes. Its current per-experiment cost is excessive for rapid development. Progress should be judged by battle strength and reliable iteration speed, not the number of sealed milestones.

## What public implementations actually offer

This is a targeted review of the most relevant primary papers, repositories and model artifacts, not an exhaustive census of all RL research. Availability was checked on October 27, 2026. Published performance remains author-reported unless reproduced locally.

| Resource | Useful contribution | Main limitation for Maxcalibur |
|---|---|---|
| [Jaxcalibur](https://jaxcalibur.github.io/) | Structured 8.5M transformer, recent events, opponent/hidden-set predictions, self-play PPO; almost 100M games in about one H100-week; search only at inference | Author [declined to release code/weights](https://www.smogon.com/forums/threads/jaxcalibur-a-gen-9-randbats-bot-that-reached-1-on-the-ladder.3787537/post-11130024). Architecture reference, not an installable agent. Most strength attributed to base policy. |
| [Metamon](https://github.com/UT-Austin-RPL/metamon) | Public learning stack, offline data, pretrained policies, self-play collection and finetuning. Documented formats are Gen1–4 OU and Gen9 OU | Random Battles transfer is unproven. Tokenizer, observation/action interface and history must stay compatible. Do not treat OU ratings as Randbats performance. |
| [Minikazam weights](https://huggingface.co/jakegrigsby/metamon/tree/main/minikazam/ckpts/policy_weights) | Actual small Metamon checkpoint files, about 19.1 MB each; a practical first transfer candidate | The 4.7M recurrent model still needs correct history and format adaptation. A small file is not proof of fast training or strong transfer. [Model configuration](https://github.com/UT-Austin-RPL/metamon/blob/main/metamon/rl/configs/models/minikazam.gin) |
| [Nebraskinator/ps-ppo](https://github.com/Nebraskinator/ps-ppo) | Direct Gen9 Random Battles reference: heuristic behavior cloning, distributed PPO, structured model and centralized batched inference | Archival code, no packaged checkpoint verified. Author reports >1900 Elo and >250M states in two RTX3090 days; not locally reproduced. README/model details differ, so reuse requires inspection. |
| [Foul Play](https://github.com/pmariglia/foul-play) | Runnable Gen9 Random Battles search opponent and possible bounded demonstration teacher | Search-based, not an RL model or ground-truth oracle. Its [poke-engine](https://github.com/pmariglia/poke-engine) backend is less complete than Showdown; keep Showdown authoritative. Client GPL-3.0, engine MIT. |
| [poke-env](https://github.com/hsahovic/poke-env) | Maintained integration, parsers and baseline players | An interface, not a stronger learner or GPU simulator. Replacing our functioning collector with it alone has no demonstrated benefit. |
| [PokéChamp](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04094v1), [PokéLLMon](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01118v2) | Useful decision, retrieval and opponent-modeling ideas | LLM inference/scaffolding methods; not cheap PPO training solutions. Different evaluation formats and timeout treatments prevent direct comparison. |
| [PokeJAX paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12145v2) | Promising accelerator-engine research with claimed transfer tests | No implementation-ready release linked from the inspected paper/author page was verified. Claimed speedups change hardware and batching; excluded edge cases remain. The separately found [Gen4 pokejax](https://github.com/JerJer2465/pokejax) is not a Gen9 replacement. |

Metamon code is MIT; [model metadata](https://huggingface.co/jakegrigsby/metamon) is Apache-2.0, and [human replay data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/jakegrigsby/metamon-parsed-replays) is CC-BY-NC-4.0. Current small and large artifacts make reuse more plausible than the original large-model paper alone suggested. Its original paper used eight A5000 GPUs, illustrating that published training-scale results are not comparable with our present local prototype. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04395v1)

The [HolidayOugi replay collection](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HolidayOugi/pokemon-showdown-replays) is another large candidate source, but the inspected page did not expose a license declaration. Public spectator logs omit private player information and sometimes selected-but-unexecuted actions. A large reconstruction effort is therefore a secondary option, not the quickest next dependency. Prefer trustworthy local demonstration trajectories for the first warm start.

Broader RL work supports these directions without guaranteeing success here: [Kickstarting](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03835) combines teacher guidance with RL; [DAgger](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html) explains why imitation accuracy need not survive closed-loop play; [R2D2](https://willdabney.com/publication/r2d2/) motivates memory while documenting recurrent-state engineering issues; [PSRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832) supports opponent mixtures and cross-play. None proves our current plateau is caused by one missing component.

## Proposed next path and stop conditions

These are proposed engineering budgets and decisions, not promises of strength or authorization to start work.

1. **Establish the external anchor first.** Cap initial Foul Play integration effort at one working day. Put it behind a local, player-information-only adapter with a fixed inference budget. Check legal protocol handling and a small full-battle panel against all three frozen F32 hosts. The deliverable is a functioning target-format benchmark and measured gap, or a concrete blocker—not an additional open-ended audit. Do not let pretrained-model integration delay this measurement. Then separately cap a small Metamon compatibility assessment at one working day: determine whether Gen9 Random Battles observations, masks, request phases and history can be adapted faithfully. Do not silently invent private fields or reuse OU performance claims. Stop transfer work at the cap if it still requires substantial interface or learner rebuilding.

2. **Create one coherent learned-policy baseline.** If the transfer candidate is compatible and useful in actual target-format battles, finetune it using its native interfaces. This imports a recurrent-state pipeline and a different learner stack; reuse only pays if those components work without extensive custom repair. Otherwise, reuse architectural ideas in our existing stack: typed embeddings for Pokémon/moves/items/abilities, numeric state, action-conditioned scoring, durable public counters and a short event window. A compact token model is enough for a first test; do not copy an 8.5M or 142M model simply to match a parameter count. For this independent-model branch, a short event window avoids introducing recurrent-state recovery immediately and the qualified PPO algorithm remains the starting point. Commit to one implementation path after the assessment, not two full training stacks.

3. **Test warm starting once, without making it a dependency for RL.** Use exact local teacher trajectories where available; a stronger teacher is a candidate, not a prerequisite that can hold the project indefinitely. Validate labels and actor information boundaries and retain source/license provenance. Retain the old failed imitation findings: this is justified by a richer representation/distribution and a stronger teacher, not a claim that more of the old rehearsal will work. Compare total acquisition/training/evaluation cost and complete-battle behavior against scratch and frozen F32 references. If acquisition or cloning does not pay within its fixed screen, continue the structured-policy RL branch rather than launching more imitation-only tuning milestones. Never feed historical demonstrations into the current PPO objective as though they were on-policy transitions. A Metamon finetune uses its own offline/online algorithm, not a checkpoint transplanted into the current PPO loss.

4. **Train against a small diverse pool and evaluate beyond it.** Include current self-play, archived checkpoints and distinct fixed opponents; keep at least one opponent family outside training. Preserve all training seeds and report per-seed outcomes. Use recurring development panels for iteration, fresh confirmation panels for claims, and the final reserve only when the design is frozen and explicitly authorized. Existing exact-team partitioning does not establish unseen-species or unseen-set generalization. Keep F33's exposed candidate identities excluded from future reserved claims.

5. **Scale collection and model together only after useful learning appears.** Profile simulation, encoding, inference, transport and writes separately. Improve persistent worker use and batched inference before attempting another full engine rewrite. Measure useful selected learner rows per second, discarded rows, terminal games, inference latency and strength per complete wall hour. Frozen-policy batches preserve PPO semantics; asynchronous collectors need explicit policy-lag control or a suitable correction, not a silent speed shortcut. [IMPALA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01561) illustrates the latter issue.

Search resumes only after stronger policy/value behavior and a credible hidden-world model make the comparison informative. F33's inconclusive result neither justifies a large search build nor proves search cannot help.

## A faster research process

For future contracts, separate development screens from confirmation. Keep deterministic fixtures, legal-action/information-boundary checks, finite-loss checks, checkpoint recovery, provenance and sampled independent game/update replay in the fast loop. Retain all failures and configurations; exploratory tuning is openly exploratory. Use fresh multi-seed confirmation with a prespecified primary battle metric for promising candidates, including regressions and uncertainty. Exhaustive replay and expensive sealing belong at agreed integration/confirmation milestones. Existing sealed requirements are not changed retroactively.

Do not turn every small inconclusive screen into a new bespoke project. The [Statistical Precipice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264) motivates reporting variability and uncertainty; a failed low-powered gate is not evidence of no effect. Plan confirmation sample size around a meaningful improvement instead of repeatedly testing effects too small for the fixed panel.

Keep a current status index outside immutable historical evidence. The index can change without invalidating old scientific records. Record one primary objective for each next milestone: actual target-format opponent strength, useful learning, or throughput—not merely another completed receipt.

## Compute and expectations

The machine exposed to this session currently reports Apple M3 Max, 14 CPU cores and 96 GiB memory, rather than the earlier M3 Pro description. [F3](F3_THROUGHPUT.md) already found the tested tiny-network Metal workload 57% slower than matched CPU; that does not predict performance for a larger model or different batch size. Keep the Mac for development, local games and integration. Rebenchmark GPU use after changing the workload. A larger NVIDIA training run is a later measured-budget decision; no resource purchase or launch is implied.

A strong agent is still a research objective, not an outcome demonstrated by this audit. The useful near-term deliverable is a credible target-format baseline and a learning curve against meaningfully stronger opposition. The existing evidence makes that a better priority than further opening-only search or repeated continuation of the current lossy encoder. Full G2/G3/G4 and public strength remain open. The GitHub visibility hold remains unchanged.

## Independent review

Three parallel read-only audits covered local trajectory, current Pokémon implementations and broader RL design. An independent Claude-family snapshot review challenged the draft's sequencing and interpretation. The final recommendation separates the external anchor from transfer integration, states that F32 has not demonstrated a plateau, retains inherited-arm differences, quantifies the replay-saving bound and makes the second learner stack an explicit transfer risk. Coordinator verification and accepted/rejected findings are recorded in the [review disposition](../artifacts/reviews/strategy-review-20261027/review-disposition.md). The reviewer did not execute code or verify live external sources; the coordinator and research agents inspected the cited sources.
